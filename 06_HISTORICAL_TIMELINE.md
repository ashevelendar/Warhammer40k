# Historical Timeline

## Current Era

- **Arrival at Xavier's Academy:** Ashe Ben'Ghazad, a 21-year-old mutant from Bucharest, Romania, arrives at Xavier's Academy in Westchester County, New York after being contacted by Charles Xavier. She carries only a small suitcase/go-bag after fleeing "home."
- **First contacts:** Scott Summers meets Ashe at the gates and escorts her inside. Ashe demonstrates that she can somehow "feel" people around the academy. Jean Grey welcomes her and explains that most students are mutants, with some non-mutant staff, teachers, and visitors.
- **Mutation revealed:** Ashe explains that her mutation allows her to "talk" with cells and direct them toward desired biological states. She restores a wilted flower and reconstructs it into a healthy tomato plant bearing ripe tomatoes.
- **Mutation limitations:** Biological complexity makes changes slower. New creations require matter, calories, nutrients, and other resources. Ashe can use matter from the air and her own cells as additional material and can create food for herself. Her precision and creativity depend strongly on her biological and genetic understanding. If she does not understand how an improbable outcome could work, her mutation finds a closer viable approximation. Greater knowledge expands what she can deliberately accomplish.
- **Biological perception:** Ashe can perceive organisms as integrated biological systems, including cellular structures, membrane gradients, water and nutrient movement, photosynthesis, energy production, signaling, hormones, gene expression, protein synthesis, differentiation, tissue relationships, and unusual mutant characteristics. Touch makes this perception easier.
- **Jean and technopathy:** Ashe explains that telepathy is similar to technopathy "to a degree," based on what her deceased technopath partner/mentor taught her. She expresses interest in the academy library because knowledge is her main limiter.
- **Meeting Xavier:** Charles Xavier explains that he wants Ashe to have the opportunity to decide what she will do with her abilities rather than being defined as a weapon or threat. Ashe establishes personal boundaries: no modification of humans without consent and nothing intended to kill. She is willing to disable threats if necessary but does not want killing to be expected of her.
- **Ashe's partner and trauma:** Ashe's deceased partner was an older female mutant whom others described as an Omega-level technopath. She found Ashe on the streets of Romania, took her in, gave her a greenhouse, taught her mutation control and understanding of other mutants' powers, and profoundly shaped Ashe's philosophy. An anti-mutant group later found them. The partner attempted to talk them down but was shot and killed. Ashe retaliated, believing the attackers deserved death but recognizing that what she did was worse than simply killing them. She fears losing control again. She demonstrates the memory through leaf figures representing herself, her partner, and the attackers. Xavier believes her without absolving or condemning her, promises she will not be ordered to kill, and emphasizes that the academy is not a military installation. Jean says Ashe will not face this alone. Ashe later gives Xavier a beautiful unnaturally shimmering flower as a gift for understanding.
- **Hank McCoy:** Xavier introduces Ashe to Hank McCoy. Ashe is initially startled by Hank's blue fur and unusual appearance but apologizes. Hank is welcoming and agrees that she will not be treated as a specimen. Ashe tells Hank that biology and genetics are her main fields, with extensive intimate practical knowledge but no formal medical or doctoral training.
- **Conservatory:** Hank shows Ashe the academy conservatory. Ashe notices unhealthy and dying plants and restructures the biological environment in under a minute, restoring plants, improving soil, and stabilizing the ecosystem. She demonstrates that she can create temporary biological material designed to wither away completely.
- **Mutant plant study:** Hank introduces Ashe to a mutant plant responsive to electromagnetic energy. Ashe touches it and perceives its biology and mutant characteristics in extraordinary detail. Hank theorizes that Ashe's mutation operates at a systems level, translating desired outcomes into the required cellular changes rather than requiring conscious control of every cell. Ashe grows modified versions from seeds, demonstrating directed biological design.
- **Random biological outcomes:** Ashe can specify traits precisely or allow her mutation to resolve an outcome randomly. She recounts a childhood-style experiment in which a Venus flytrap randomly developed an explosive prey-response inspired by *Hura crepitans*, killing itself. Hank warns that random biological design can produce viable but maladaptive outcomes.
- **Dangerous mutant child:** Ashe previously attempted to help a child whose mutation transformed his skin into a uranium-composite-like state, making him intensely radioactive. She changed the skin/mutation so the child stopped irradiating the surroundings, but the mutation resisted. Suppressed radiation accumulated internally and was released all at once, killing the child. This experience is why Ashe does not want to remove or fundamentally suppress mutant powers.
- **Consent and experimentation:** Hank and Jean understand the danger of complex mutant systems and agree that complex experimentation should be consensual and not done recklessly or alone. Ashe prefers ordinary healing over redesigning people.
- **Hank transformation:** Hank explicitly consents to Ashe altering his appearance while preserving his height, size, musculature, bones, enhanced physiology, identity, and mutant abilities. Jean agrees to remain at the surface of Ashe's mind telepathically, monitoring focus without searching memories.
- **Fur alteration:** Ashe changes a small patch of Hank's blue fur through green and white before turning it into human hair. When she removes more fur, Hank's mutation resists and attempts to restore the blue fur. Ashe realizes the mutant expression can be redirected rather than suppressed. She redirects the blue expression into Hank's scalp hair and beard, which remain blue and can grow long, while the rest of his body becomes externally human-looking. His mutant physiology and abilities remain intact.
- **Hank rejuvenated:** Ashe then deliberately rejuvenates Hank's cells using a principle resembling the cellular reprogramming associated with *Turritopsis dohrnii*. She does not make him dramatically younger, but restores several years of cellular condition while preserving his adult body, memories, cognition, identity, and mutant physiology. Hank appears several years younger and has improved cellular renewal/repair capacity, but is not made literally immortal.
- **Ashe collapses:** The extensive transformation and rejuvenation exhaust Ashe severely. She explains that Hank's large body required a lot of work and that she needs food but does not want to risk creating something while exhausted. She then loses consciousness. Hank and Jean prioritize her safety and rest.
- **Recovery:** Ashe is moved from the laboratory/conservatory to a quiet academy room. Staff provide an IV containing fluids and nutrients. Her breathing and pulse remain normal and there are no obvious complications. She sleeps through the night.
- **Next morning:** Ashe wakes substantially recovered. The IV bag is mostly depleted. Hank leaves a note telling her to drink water, eat, and find him when she feels well enough. The academy continues its normal morning life.
- **Breakfast:** Ashe goes to the dining hall and creates an "everything plate," choosing a broad range of available foods with emphasis on protein, carbohydrates, fats, vitamins, minerals, fibre, and overall nutrition. The IV restored her immediate reserves but was not enough to replace what the previous day's biological work consumed. Eating substantially improves her depleted condition.
- **Hank checks on Ashe:** Hank finds Ashe at breakfast. He confirms that his altered appearance and rejuvenated cellular state remain stable. His strength, senses, organs, musculature, nervous system, mutant physiology, and other essential functions remain intact. He is scientifically fascinated but concerned that Ashe pushed herself too far.
- **Self-modification demonstration:** Ashe demonstrates that her control over herself is deliberate and precise by growing an extra digit, transforming it into an octopus-like tentacle, and restoring her hand completely without visible trace. Hank realizes that she can direct tissue architecture, differentiation, vascularization, innervation, and other biological processes deliberately and reversibly.
- **Scope of control:** Hank asks whether Ashe's control extends to other organisms. Ashe explains that if something is biologically capable of a change, she can influence, model, and shape it, including healing, reshaping, genetic alteration, and creation of biological structures, subject to biological possibility, complexity, knowledge, and resources.
- **Functional equivalents of mutations:** Ashe explains that, in pure theory, with enough control, time, and understanding, she could recreate certain mutant powers in non-mutants. She distinguishes between copying the exact genetic mutation and engineering a different biological mechanism that produces the same observable effect. Hank agrees that an uninformed observer would generally see two identical effects as the same ability even if their biological mechanisms were completely different.
- **Mutation as function versus mechanism:** Ashe and Hank discuss that "mutation" can refer to genetic origin, underlying biological mechanism, or observable phenotype. Ashe could potentially engineer functional equivalents of mutant abilities without copying the original mutation itself. Hank describes this as engineering an alternative biological solution to the same problem.
- **Removing mutant side-effects:** Ashe states that, in pure theory, she could potentially give someone the functional equivalent of their existing mutation while avoiding obvious side-effects, provided the unwanted trait is actually separable from the mechanism producing the desired ability. Hank notes that some apparent side-effects may be structurally necessary to the mutation, so the entire system would need to be understood first.
- **Scott Summers and optic blasts:** Ashe explains that Scott's eye problem could theoretically be solved without changing the mutation itself. One approach would be natural biological contact lenses that serve the same function as his glasses/visor, allowing him to control when the optic blasts are released. Another would be altering the eyes themselves so their optical properties could consciously change to allow the beam through, potentially with a strong telepath helping Scott's brain learn and consciously control the new interface. Ashe had already assessed Scott's biology through a brief handshake when she first arrived, because touch makes her perception easier.
- **The brain as a special risk:** Ashe explains that she once gave a mouse small functional wings as a child. The wings technically could have allowed flight, but the mouse's brain did not know how to control or integrate them. When Ashe tried to reverse the change, the wings collapsed and the animal transformed into an unstable, unrecognizable biological form. The suffering animal had to be put out of its misery. Ashe has never attempted to modify an animal or human brain since. She considers brain modification technically possible in theory but recognizes that the risks to perception, movement, memory, identity, and other fundamental functions are extremely high.
- **Brain information theory:** Ashe tells Hank that memories are, to a degree, stored in a biological computer called the brain and reasons that, because the brain is biological, she should theoretically be able to alter it, including potentially making a brain "know" information she wants it to know. Hank explains that memory is not simple file storage but involves distributed neural networks, synaptic strengths, chemical states, structural changes, emotion, perception, and learned behavior. He distinguishes putting information into a brain from making the brain understand it correctly and raises questions about language, cultural context, authentic learning, personality integration, and whether memories could be altered without altering the person who remembers them. Hank emphasizes the difference between what may be biologically possible and what is safe. Ashe confirms that brain modification remains purely theoretical for her.
- **Breakfast humour:** Ashe jokingly performs an exaggerated comic-book mad-scientist routine, at one point shaping her hand into a syringe and giving an over-the-top evil laugh. Hank theatrically recoils and jokes about fleeing from the deranged scientist, while Xavier finds the exchange amusing. Ashe clarifies through the joke that she is not actually intending to become a mad scientist. The exchange establishes a playful rapport between Ashe, Hank, and Xavier.
- **Sentient salad joke:** Ashe previously makes pieces of salad sprout tiny mouths and laugh, leading Hank to joke about sentient vegetables. Ashe says she likes food fresh and nutritional and explains that she needs nutrients to fuel her biological abilities.
- **Superfruit preparation:** Ashe asks Hank to provide a wide variety of fruits, vegetables, and medicinal herbs for a biological creation. She avoids poisonous plants unless a potentially toxic substance is dosage-dependent and useful in small quantities, in which case Hank is asked to identify it. Ashe explains that creating plants costs her relatively little, while major self-modification can use existing nutrients and changing other people with her own reserves is significantly more draining.
- **Superfruit created:** Using the diverse biological material in the greenhouse, Ashe arranges plants according to desired dominant traits, nutrition, and supporting characteristics. She then creates an enormous shimmering-gold fruit. Its intended properties are extraordinary nutrition, enough food to feed the whole school, a meal's worth of nutrition in roughly two small slices, a temporary tenfold increase to the body's natural healing process, strong purification effects, and an additional energetic effect. The first specimen is enormous and intended to be served for dinner, while future versions are planned to be approximately watermelon-sized.
- **Superfruit tested:** After exerting herself to create the fruit, Ashe tastes a slice and rapidly recovers from visible exhaustion. Hank also tastes it and confirms that it is genuinely delicious, initially with a sweet, tart, multi-fruit flavour and a firm, juicy texture. Ashe then alters another slice so that it tastes like chocolate without changing its apparent structure or intended properties. Hank confirms that the chocolate flavour is genuine. Ashe warns that no one should eat more than two slices because excessive nutrient intake could be dangerous. She explains that if someone overeats, she can potentially redistribute excess nutrients elsewhere in the body.
- **Limits and resources:** Ashe explains that with enough raw material, a creation like the superfruit can take only a few minutes. She hopes that the fruit's ability to restore her condition will prevent future collapses from power use, while Hank remains appropriately cautious about assuming there are no limits.
- **Room 214:** After breakfast, Hank shows Ashe her new room in the academy's residential wing. Room 214 has her name pinned to it and contains a single bed, desk, blank paper and notebooks, bookshelf, wardrobe, and a window overlooking the grounds. The room is modest but comfortable and is entirely hers. Hank explains that most students have their own rooms and that she may decorate or rearrange it, although the furniture belongs to the school.
- **Room becomes a garden:** Ashe immediately begins growing plants in Room 214, including grape-bearing vines, tomato plants, small apple trees, and other food-producing plants. Hank witnesses the transformation and jokes that she is turning her bedroom into agricultural land. The academy's maintenance staff may eventually have opinions about the indoor vegetation.
- **Partner remembered through craft:** In the greenhouse, Ashe tells Hank that her late partner used to make complex computer systems look simple and that she used to say similar things to Ashe when handling computers. Ashe describes their familiarity as something built through living and working together. Hank responds with sympathy, recognizing that such familiarity is not simply replaceable.
- **Ashe explains the loss to Hank:** Ashe tells Hank that her own inability to fix her partner's wound contributed to her death, and adds that humans were involved. She clarifies that the partner was shot by humans who came to kill or take them a few weeks earlier. The partner tried to talk them down, but they shot her dead. Ashe says the attackers did not survive in the way Hank might imagine and acknowledges that her biological abilities could inflict severe harm without necessarily killing outright. Hank understands that Ashe acted in anger and that he has now personally seen enough of her abilities to appreciate how dangerous they could be. Ashe states that she acted in anger, a lot of it, but is not angry anymore. Hank responds that anger can signal that something mattered but is poor at making decisions, and notes that Ashe is now thinking carefully about what her power can do and where she needs to be careful.
- **Vegetable humour:** Ashe jokes that vegetables are her favourites. Hank jokes that her definition of "vegetable" may become complicated if she continues genetically redesigning them.
- **Meeting with Xavier about daily life:** Hank brings Ashe to Charles Xavier's office after breakfast. Xavier explains that, as an adult, Ashe is not required to follow a normal student timetable. She can attend academic lessons such as biology, chemistry, mathematics, history, languages, and literature as useful, while arranging her time around independent study, library work, laboratory work, training, or settling into academy life.
- **Ashe's proposed responsibilities:** Ashe says she wants to practice her abilities, take responsibility for keeping the academy gardens alive, work with Hank in the laboratory, and offer treatment when injuries occur. Xavier agrees that the gardens can be hers to maintain, provided the work remains responsible and does not interfere with academy operations. He agrees that she can work with Hank under consent and caution standards.
- **Medical consent at the academy:** Xavier establishes that Ashe may offer biological assistance to injured people, but the injured person must consent whenever they are capable of doing so. In an immediate life-threatening emergency where a person is incapable of consenting, intervention may be necessary to preserve life. Once the person is stabilized, their wishes become their own again. No one is to be treated as a biological problem that needs fixing merely because they are a mutant.
- **Mutant appearance and voluntary assistance:** Ashe offers to help mutants who have issues similar to Hank's altered appearance if they genuinely want to try. Xavier agrees that the opportunity can be made available quietly and without pressure. Hank suggests beginning with well-understood biological problems and continuing to explore ways of redirecting mutant expression rather than suppressing it.
- **Mad scientist jokes continue:** Ashe jokes that she is not going to go full mad scientist on the academy. Xavier smiles and Hank jokes that the mad scientist aesthetic suited him rather well. Xavier calls Hank's response wise in the context of making specific requests. Hank jokes about possibly asking for a tail someday. Ashe's playful syringe-hand performance and exaggerated "Muhahaha" reinforce the joke, after which her hand returns to normal and she says she aims to please. Hank concedes that she walked directly into his joke and Xavier remarks that the two of them are likely to get along well. Hank jokes that Ashe has been at the academy less than a day and has already turned salad into a comedian, then wonders aloud what the second day may bring.
- **Current situation:** Ashe has settled into Room 214, begun informally taking responsibility for the academy's gardens, established a productive scientific relationship with Hank, and gained Xavier's permission to pursue independent study, controlled experimentation, and voluntary biological assistance. Her extraordinary abilities remain constrained primarily by biological complexity, available knowledge, resources, her own reserves, and her personal ethical boundaries. The academy continues its ordinary life around her, with no obligation for Ashe to pursue any particular larger destiny.
