# Historical Timeline

## Current Era
- **Autumn, current year:** Ashe Ben'Ghazad, a 21-year-old mutant from Bucharest, Romania, arrives at Xavier's Academy in Westchester County, New York after being contacted by Charles Xavier.
- **Arrival at the academy:** Ashe arrives carrying only a small suitcase/go-bag, explaining to Scott Summers that she had to flee "home" and had little time to gather belongings.
- **First contact with Scott Summers:** Scott meets Ashe at the academy gates, confirms her identity, introduces himself, and begins escorting her toward the mansion. Ashe demonstrates that she can somehow "feel" the people around the academy, though the nature and limits of this perception remain unexplained.
- **First contact with Jean Grey:** Jean comes outside carrying books. Scott introduces Ashe as the new arrival. Jean welcomes Ashe to Xavier's Academy.
- **Mutation discussion:** Ashe explains that her mutation allows her to "talk" with cells and direct them toward desired biological states. She demonstrates rapid restoration of a wilted flower, then reconstructs the flower into a healthy tomato plant bearing ripe tomatoes.
- **Mutation limitations discussed:** Ashe explains that biological complexity makes modification slower, while genuinely new creations also depend on available matter, caloric intake, nutrients, and other physical resources. She notes that she can use matter from the air and her own cells as additional material and can create food for herself.
- **Telepathy and technopathy discussion:** Ashe describes telepathy as similar to technopathy "to a degree," based on what her deceased technopath partner/mentor taught her. Jean and Scott learn that Ashe has significant prior experience thinking about and using her mutation.
- **Academy discussion:** Jean explains that most students at the academy are mutants, while some teachers, staff, and visitors may not be. Ashe expresses interest in the academy's large library and says she has had her mutation for a long time and believes knowledge is its main limiter.
- **Professor Xavier:** Ashe says she understands why Professor Xavier contacted her and asks Jean to show her where he is, believing he has been expecting her. Jean confirms that Xavier is in his study and offers to take Ashe there.
- **Brief student interruption:** Before they leave, a young male student calls down from the upper landing and welcomes Ashe to the mansion. Jean and Scott treat the interruption as ordinary academy life, with Scott telling the student to do his homework. Ashe acknowledges the student with a wave and thank-you nod.
- **Meeting with Xavier:** Xavier explains that Ashe's extraordinary power is only part of why he invited her. He wants her to have the opportunity to decide what she will do with her abilities rather than allowing others to define her as a weapon or threat. He offers the academy as a place where she can learn, develop her abilities, and live among people who understand unusual mutations.
- **Ashe's boundaries:** Ashe explains that she already has substantial biological knowledge because of her mutation and has established personal rules: no modification of humans without consent, and nothing intended to kill. She acknowledges that greater knowledge could theoretically allow her to do "way more" with her abilities. She also makes clear that she does not want to be forced to kill people. If necessary, she is willing to disable threats, but does not want killing to be expected of her.
- **The memory of her partner:** Ashe explains that her deceased partner was an older female mutant and an Omega-level technopath, according to what others called her. The woman found Ashe on the streets of Romania, took her in, gave her a greenhouse, taught her to control and understand her mutation, and helped her understand other mutants' abilities. Ashe describes her as "everything" and says her own life philosophy was shaped by her partner's teachings.
- **The attack:** Ashe recounts that an anti-mutant group tracked her and her partner down. Her partner attempted to talk them down, but they shot and killed her without warning. Ashe admits that she retaliated and that the attackers "deserved death" in her view, but what she did to them was worse than killing. She carries both hatred and shame over the incident and fears that if similar people come for her again, she might lose control badly enough to kill not only the attackers but everyone around her.
- **Leaf reconstruction demonstration:** While recounting the attack, Ashe grows a branch of a nearby tree toward the study window, takes several leaves, and reconstructs them into small figures representing herself, her partner, and the anti-mutant attackers. The figures act out the events she described. The attacker figures remain non-animated after the demonstration.
- **Xavier's response:** Xavier does not condemn Ashe or absolve what happened. He tells her that he believes her and recognizes that her fear of losing control is meaningful. He makes clear that she will not be ordered to kill for him and that the academy is not a military installation. He says that learning how to remain herself when given every reason not to is as important as learning to control or expand her mutation. Jean reinforces that Ashe will not have to figure this out alone.
- **A gift to Xavier:** After Xavier's reassurance, Ashe grows the leaves into a single beautiful flower and reconstructs it into an unnaturally shimmering color that could not occur naturally. She presents it to Xavier as a gift "for understanding." Xavier accepts the gesture warmly.
- **Meeting Hank McCoy:** Xavier takes Ashe to the academy's laboratory to meet Hank McCoy. Ashe is initially startled by Hank's blue fur and unusual appearance, then apologizes and explains that she was simply caught by surprise. Hank responds warmly, gives her space, and reassures her that he will not treat her like a specimen.
- **Ashe's scientific interests:** Ashe tells Hank that biology and genetics are her primary fields. She explains that she has extensive intimate knowledge of genetics and biological systems, though it is not equivalent to formal medical or doctoral training. Hank becomes highly interested in her practical understanding of biology and her ability to perceive and manipulate living systems.
- **Garden conditions and reconstruction:** Hank shows Ashe the academy's conservatory and explains that it contains research plants and specimens. Ashe immediately notices that several plants are unhealthy, some have inadequate conditions, and some are dying. With her mutation, she restructures the entire conservatory in under a minute: plants recover, vines grow, the soil is improved, and the overall environment becomes healthy and stable. She explains that she changed the entire biological environment rather than merely repairing individual plants.
- **Temporary botanical creations:** Ashe explains that the grass she creates can be deliberately designed to wither within minutes, leaving nothing behind. She demonstrates this in the laboratory, creating a circular patch of grass that later begins disappearing as intended.
- **Boundaries around human modification:** Ashe tells Hank that she prefers not to modify people. Restoring ordinary biological processes after injuries is acceptable to her, but she does not want to actively redesign human bodies or create additional structures such as new bones except where necessary to heal. Hank accepts this boundary as reasonable and agrees that any experimentation involving other people must require informed consent.
- **Mutant botanical specimen:** Hank introduces Ashe to a mutant plant whose mutation causes it to respond to electromagnetic energy. Ashe approaches it cautiously and says that she had already felt it before touching it. Hank permits her to make contact while asking her to describe what she perceives before deliberately changing anything.
- **Ashe's biological perception:** Upon touching the mutant plant, Ashe perceives its biological organization in extraordinary detail. Hank interprets her descriptions as awareness of the organism as an integrated biological system rather than conscious manipulation of individual cells. The observations include cellular structures, membrane gradients, water and nutrient movement, photosynthesis, energy production, signaling, hormonal activity, gene expression, protein synthesis, cellular differentiation, and relationships between tissues. Ashe can also perceive that the plant possesses unusual characteristics associated with its mutation.
- **Possible systems-level mutation control:** Based on Ashe's description, Hank theorizes that her mutation does not require her to consciously control every cell. Instead, it may perceive the organism's overall biological state and translate her intended outcome into the necessary cellular changes. He becomes interested in whether Ashe's perception extends beyond ordinary physiology to the properties that make mutant organisms unusual.
- **Directed biological design:** Ashe experiments with seeds from the mutant plant, placing them into several pots and using her mutation to grow different versions of the organism. Each plant receives a different trait or minor modification, including differences in coloration and altered biological behavior. Hank observes that Ashe is not merely modifying an existing organism's phenotype but can apparently influence reproductive material and produce differing offspring-like forms.
- **Design versus randomness:** Ashe explains to Hank that she can either specify traits very precisely according to her intent or allow the mutation to resolve the outcome with a degree of randomness. She notes that random modifications are inherently unpredictable. As an example, she recalls once allowing a Venus flytrap to develop randomly and having it acquire an explosive prey-response similar in principle to the seed dispersal of *Hura crepitans*, causing captured prey to trigger an internal explosion that killed the plant itself.
- **Hank's interpretation of randomness:** Hank reasons that Ashe's "random" biological design may represent an enormous search through possible biological outcomes constrained by available matter, energy, developmental pathways, and existing biological architecture. He warns that unconstrained experimentation could produce viable but dangerously maladaptive solutions, emphasizing the importance of careful observation and documentation.
- **Hank's current interest:** Hank becomes increasingly convinced that Ashe's mutation may not simply reconstruct existing biology but may be capable of biological design. He is particularly interested in determining whether Ashe consciously imagines a complete finished organism or merely specifies an intended trait and allows her mutation to determine the biological pathways required to achieve it.
- **Hank's laboratory arrangement:** Ashe asks to conduct her own experiments and to use the academy's library, preferably while reading in a garden. Hank agrees to let her conduct her own experiments provided they are safe and that experiments involving other people require informed consent. The library is available to students. Hank also offers the conservatory for her use and invites her to bring interesting discoveries to him.
- **Current direction:** Ashe is now in Hank McCoy's laboratory/conservatory and has established a friendly scientific rapport with him. Hank is eager to investigate the boundaries of Ashe's mutation alongside her rather than treating her as a specimen.
